## Lessons
- **Error dilution:** a ratio over *all* traffic hides an endpoint that is badly broken but small. 20 % of orders failing looked like 5 % overall. Options: alert per route (`sum by (route)`), or alert on the most important endpoint separately (an SLO for "checkout"). Trade-off: per-route alerts on low-traffic routes are noisy.
- **Thresholds right at the normal value flap.** Pending → inactive → pending means the condition crossed the line back and forth; `for: 1m` restarted every time, so nobody was paged.
- **`rate(...[1m])` needs time to fill up:** it took 139 s until the ratio first crossed 5 %, plus `for: 1m` before firing.
- **Histogram percentiles are estimates:** `histogram_quantile` only knows how many requests fell into each bucket and interpolates linearly inside the bucket. 800 ms showed up as ~0.99 s because the buckets are 0.75 and 1.0. Precision depends on the bucket boundaries: put boundaries near your SLO threshold (e.g. 0.5 s).
- **Latency spreads, errors don't:** latency hit every route at once, so there was no dilution and the alert fired in 75 s. Slow requests also reduce throughput and raise in-flight requests (Little's law: in flight ≈ rate × duration, 9.8 × ~0.8 ≈ 8).
- **Alerts lag both ways:** they need `rate[1m]` + `for: 1m` to fire, and about the window length (60 s) to resolve after the fix.
- **Exceptions must not skip your instrumentation (exp 5):** wrap `call_next` so that the count, the access log and `in_progress.dec()` also run when the endpoint raises. Otherwise the dashboard shows *less traffic* during an outage instead of *errors*, and a gauge drifts forever. "Watch the watchers": test the failure path of your monitoring.
- **Liveness vs readiness (exp 5):** `/health` stayed 200 while `/ready` was 503. Good: restarting the api wouldn't fix a dead database, so Kubernetes (M6) should stop sending traffic, not restart the pod.
- **Why 500 for a dead database:** the client did nothing wrong (so not 4xx); it's the server's failure. 503 would be even more precise ("temporarily unavailable, retry later").
- **Silent failures (exp 3):** an OOM restart was invisible to all three alerts, because the restart was faster than one scrape. In Kubernetes, kube-state-metrics provides `kube_pod_container_status_restarts_total` and the reason `OOMKilled` to alert on.
- **`for:` works (exp 4):** a 4-second crash-and-restart went pending, never paged.
- **Find one request:** Grafana Explore → Loki → `{service="api"} |= "<request_id>"`.
